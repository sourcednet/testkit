On keeping things

Every archive begins as a decision about what is worth keeping, and every archive ends up keeping far more than anyone decided. This essay follows one small community archive over several decades, from a shelf of donated letters to a collection that fills three buildings, and asks what its keepers learned along the way.

What the keepers said

We never had a policy for most of what came through the door. We had a habit of saying yes, and a habit of writing down why.

The keepers' own notes, kept in plain notebooks next to the accession registers, turned out to be the most valuable record of all. They explain choices that the catalog alone never could.

What they learned

  • Write down why something was kept, not only what it is.
  • Keep the first description, even after it is corrected.
  • Decide early who may read what, and record the decision.
  • Expect every rule to meet a case it did not foresee.
