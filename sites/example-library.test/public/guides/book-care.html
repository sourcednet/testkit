Caring for old books

Old books last for centuries when they are stored and handled with a little care. Most damage comes from a few everyday causes: light, damp or dry air, careless handling, and pests. This guide covers the simple habits that prevent almost all of it, for home collections and small libraries alike. None of it needs special equipment, only attention to where books live and how they are used.

Light

Light fades spines and covers and weakens paper over time. Ultraviolet light from the sun and from some older lamps does the most harm, but visible light adds up too. Keep shelves out of direct sunlight, draw curtains in rooms where valuable books are kept, and prefer LED lighting, which gives off very little ultraviolet. Rotate books on display every few months so no single volume takes all the exposure.

Humidity

Keep the relative humidity where books are stored between 60 and 70 percent. Paper, leather, and glue all respond to moisture in the air, so the goal is a steady level rather than a perfect number. Sudden swings cause more harm than a level that is slightly off, because materials expand and shrink at different rates and bindings crack under the strain.

A simple hygrometer shows the humidity in a room and costs very little. Check it through the seasons, since heating in winter can make air very dry and summer can bring damp. Avoid storing books in basements, attics, or against outside walls, where moisture and temperature change the most.

Handling

  • Wash and dry your hands before handling old books.
  • Pull a book from the shelf by its sides, never by the top of the spine.
  • Support the covers when opening a fragile binding.
  • Use a slip of acid-free paper as a bookmark.
