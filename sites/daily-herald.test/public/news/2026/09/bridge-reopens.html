Old stone bridge reopens after repairs

The old stone bridge over the river reopened to traffic on Monday morning after eight months of repairs. Engineers replaced the worn mortar between the arches, rebuilt a section of the eastern parapet, and added drainage under the road surface so that water no longer collects on the deck after heavy rain.

The repairs cost 2.4 million, slightly under the budget approved by the town council last winter. Most of the money came from a regional fund for historic infrastructure, with the town covering the rest from its maintenance reserve.

What changes for drivers

The bridge is open to cars and bicycles in both directions. Trucks over 7.5 tonnes remain banned, as they were before the repairs, and the speed limit on the bridge stays at 30 km/h. The temporary ferry service that ran during the works stopped on Sunday evening.

Residents on both banks welcomed the reopening. Shop owners near the eastern end said the months of detours had cut their trade by nearly a third, and several said they hoped customers would return quickly now that the crossing is short again.
